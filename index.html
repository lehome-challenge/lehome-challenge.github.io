Overview

Deformable object manipulation remains one of robotics' toughest open problems, essential for robots to work in real households. LeHome Challenge 2027 focuses this problem on cooking-style kitchen tasks, from handling utensils to manipulating soft food ingredients. The competition is simulation-driven: teams first develop and validate their solutions in simulation, then deploy the best-performing methods on real robots.

01 Sim-Ready Kitchen Environments & Assets
Standardized simulated kitchen scenes with a fixed set of manipulable assets, for recipe-driven hamburger and sandwich tasks with controlled variation.
02 Diverse Manipulation Strategies
Open to modular perception-planning-control pipelines and end-to-end policies alike, including imitation learning, reinforcement learning, VLA, and World Action Models.
03 Evaluation in Both Simulation and the Real World
A two-stage pipeline: an online Simulation Challenge qualifying round, then a Real-World Challenge on a common robotic platform for top teams.

Rule

A two-stage pipeline: all teams enter the online Simulation Challenge, and the six highest-ranked teams advance to the on-site Real-World Challenge.

Standardized Environment & Asset

The Simulation Challenge runs on a common robot-sensor setup shared by all finalist teams. The Real-World Challenge uses safe plastic and silicone replicas matching the geometry and physics of their simulation counterparts.

Benchmark Variation

Positional

Ingredient positions and orientations vary within the reachable workspace.

Visual

Lighting, tabletop and backdrop appearance, and non-interactive background objects vary while the robot–camera geometry stays fixed.

Recipe

Ingredient combinations and target layer orders vary across hamburger and sandwich recipes.

Scoring

Each recipe scores one point per consecutively correct layer from the bottom bun — a mistake at layer k+1 caps the score at k. Rankings go by total score, then completed tasks, then execution time as a tiebreaker.
